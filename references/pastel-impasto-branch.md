# Branch B: Pastel Impasto Pastoral

Use this branch for airy hand-painted posters and structured editorial infographics. For every Branch B request, use `../assets/branch-b-reference.jpg` as the single visual reference. It controls palette, paint handling, atmosphere, and layered spatial rhythm for both modes. The shepherd, sheep, and landscape are example content only; do not reproduce them unless the user's subject calls for them.

## Visual DNA

### Palette

- Primary field: powder sky blue, misty aqua, pale mint, warm ivory.
- Painted accents: blush pink, soft coral, buttercream, muted periwinkle, and small warm underpainting flecks.
- Ground and lower fields: sage, muted moss, khaki, sand, restrained ochre, and dusty blue.
- Typography and linework: dark desaturated slate blue or blue-charcoal.
- Avoid Branch A's brick-red dominance, harsh black fields, neon, and high-gloss color gradients.

### Paint and surface

- Use visible oil-paint impasto: layered palette-knife ridges, broad opaque slabs, dry dragged edges, creamy pigment build-up, and occasional soft scrape-through.
- Build calm, interlocking painted color fields with visible canvas tooth and hints of warm underpainting—not paper collage, broken plaster, grunge noise, ink-loss, or peeling letters.
- Keep thickest paint on the central subject or headline. Let surrounding text fields and dense diagram cells remain quieter and legible.
- The work may include small pigment flecks, but they must read as paint deposits, never as weathering or damage.

### Spatial rhythm

- Learn from the reference's large open sky, narrow distant horizon, and stacked horizontal land bands. Use this rhythm when it supports the requested layout; do not force a landscape scene into unrelated subject matter.
- Favor a few large quiet color fields over many decorative fragments. Small figures or motifs can provide scale, but the atmosphere and paint structure should carry the image.
- Treat the reference as a style/material source, not a template whose exact composition must be copied.

### Subject treatment

- Subjects remain recognizable, but become painterly abstractions: simplify forms into broad planes, restrained silhouettes, and purposeful color blocks.
- Avoid fussy anatomy, photographic surface detail, or hyperreal textures unless the user explicitly asks for realism.
- When a large motif deconstructs, transition deliberately between color planes, construction linework, faceted geometry, halftone, collage, or motion traces.

## Poster route

Use for lyrical posters with one dominant subject.

- Favor the supplied aspect ratio; when none is given, choose the ratio that best fits the subject. Preserve an airy quiet field, an off-center or lower-center focal motif, and a few layered planes.
- A useful pattern is a quiet vertical Chinese title zone on the left, a modest English subtitle beneath, then one large painterly subject with two or three small echo motifs.
- Use broad sky-toned panels and low layered bands for depth without photographic perspective, even when the subject is not a literal landscape.
- Typography is calm and precise: dark slate Chinese characters with generous spacing, a refined serif Latin companion, thin rules, and an uncluttered text field. Do not use Branch A's chipped letterpress.

## Infographic route

Use for a data-rich or typological chart that needs the same light pastel material language while remaining clearly editorial.

- Keep the hierarchy rational: a large title and hero study in the upper half, an exact grid or sequence in the middle/lower portion, and a restrained material strip or legend at the base.
- Hero titles may be deep blue-charcoal impasto letterforms with visible fresh paint ridges. They are dense, legible painted letters—not distressed print, missing ink, or erosion.
- For a “50 visual states” study, retain an exact 5-row × 10-column matrix with continuous numbering and five conceptual groups. Use a stylized hero subject that can move from broad paint planes to construction linework.
- Small specimens must remain non-photoreal. Vary them by group: silhouettes/geometric forms; wireframes/structure; collage/halftone/surface; motion traces; symbolic emblems. Keep each specimen recognizably related to the subject.
- Use more deliberate paint and contrast in the hero, flatter graphic studies in the matrix, and quieter palette samples at the base.

## Validation

- Confirm the selected branch matches the request before generating.
- Check that Branch B titles read as fresh paint with volume, not as damaged printing.
- For infographics, verify every requested grid cell, number, group boundary, and text field after generation; repair only named cells when the rest is correct.
- Confirm `branch-b-reference.jpg` is the only Branch B style reference attached to the generation request.
- Reject a result if it drifts into photoreal illustration, gritty wall damage, or Branch A's weathered mural vocabulary.
